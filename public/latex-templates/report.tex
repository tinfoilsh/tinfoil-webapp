\begin{keybox}{Summary}
Checkout p95 latency has risen from 480\,ms to 910\,ms over two quarters. We propose three changes: a read-through cache for the pricing service, batching of inventory checks, and moving tax calculation off the request path. Expected result: p95 below 400\,ms by the end of Q1 at a cost of roughly 6 engineer-weeks.
\end{keybox}

\section{Background}
Checkout latency is the strongest predictor of cart abandonment in our data. Each 100\,ms of additional p95 latency corresponds to a 0.6\,\% drop in conversion. Over the last two quarters, latency has grown in step with catalogue size and with the number of synchronous calls made per request.

\section{Options considered}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{6.4cm}rr@{}}\toprule
\lab{Option} & \lab{Description} & \lab{Effort} & \lab{p95 gain}\\\midrule
A & Cache pricing responses (TTL 60\,s) & 2 wk & $-220$\,ms\\
B & Batch inventory checks per cart & 2.5 wk & $-180$\,ms\\
C & Async tax calculation with fallback & 1.5 wk & $-140$\,ms\\
D & Rewrite checkout service in Go & 14 wk & $-450$\,ms\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\subsection{Recommendation}
Options A, B and C together deliver most of the benefit of D at a fraction of the cost and can ship independently. We recommend starting with A, which also reduces load on the pricing database.

\section{Plan}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Weeks 1--2:} pricing cache behind a feature flag; shadow traffic to validate hit rate.
  \item \textbf{Weeks 3--5:} batched inventory API; migrate the cart service.
  \item \textbf{Weeks 5--6:} async tax path with synchronous fallback for regulated regions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Risks}
Stale prices during promotions are the main risk of option A; we mitigate it with explicit cache invalidation on price updates. Option C depends on the tax provider's webhook reliability, which we will measure during week 1.
